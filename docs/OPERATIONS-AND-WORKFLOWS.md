Human Safety Foundation is a Bangladesh-based:

- Non-governmental organization

# 8. Monthly Fund Requisition Workflow

## 8.1 Purpose of the MFR

The Monthly Fund Requisition is the principal document used to request funds for project activities and office operations.

Each project coordinator prepares an MFR at the beginning of the month.

Separate MFRs are prepared for:

- E4BL
- A2PHC
- Climate Action Initiative
- Women Empowerment Initiative
- Other approved projects when required

A separate special MFR is prepared for:

- Central Office Management

## 8.2 MFR preparation

The project coordinator:

1. Reviews the annual plan and approved budget.
2. Identifies activities planned for the month.
3. Identifies salary, operational, field, event, training, and purchase requirements.
4. Selects the relevant account heads.
5. Prepares detailed annexes.
6. Calculates the requested amount.
7. Writes the amount in words.
8. Submits the MFR for budget checking and approval.

## 8.3 Existing MFR structure

The sample MFRs contain:

- Organization name
- Form title
- Request date
- Serial number
- Project name
- Working area
- Month
- Project period
- Requested amount
- Amount in words
- Account-head sections
- Amount column
- Remarks
- Annex references
- Grand total
- Requested-by section
- Finance budget-checking section
- Recommendation section
- Approval section
- Names, designations, signatures, and dates
- A note requiring the budgeted amount or special recommendation

## 8.4 MFR categories

The sample form separates costs into:

- Capital Expenditure
- Office Operation Cost
- Project Implementation Cost

The placement of salaries under Capital Expenditure is not conventional accounting treatment and should be corrected later.

## 8.5 MFR annex

The annex provides line-level details such as:

- Serial number
- Item or person
- Quantity
- Unit
- Unit cost
- Total
- Remarks
- Location
- Payment mode, when included

The main MFR links the account-head totals from the annex.

## 8.6 Sample MFR values

The November 2025 samples show:

| MFR                       | Historical sample request |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------: |
| A2PHC                     |               BDT 332,004 |
| Central Office Management |               BDT 494,590 |
| E4BL                      |               BDT 169,690 |
| **Combined**              |           **BDT 996,284** |

These figures are historical samples, not current approved budgets.

## 8.7 Approval chain

The current approval path is broadly:

```text
Project Coordinator / Requester
        ↓
Finance Budget Checking
        ↓
CEO or Senior Management Recommendation
        ↓
Chairman / Authorized Final Approver
        ↓
Fund Disbursement
```

The exact approval authority may vary by amount, project, donor restriction, or type of expenditure. A formal approval matrix is not yet documented.

## 8.8 Additional MFR

When an unplanned or additional cost is required:

1. The field office contacts the project coordinator.
2. The request may be made by:
   - Phone
   - Email
   - WhatsApp message
3. The project coordinator reviews the need.
4. The coordinator prepares an Additional MFR.
5. The Additional MFR goes through approval.
6. The additional funds are collected.
7. The funds are disbursed to the field.

The current communication may occur informally before formal approval. The future system must preserve urgency while creating a documented audit trail.

## 8.9 MFR control requirement

Every expense is expected to be:

- Against an approved MFR
- Within an approved account head
- Supported by proper bills and vouchers
- Within the approved amount unless an Additional MFR exists

# 9. Purchase Requisition and Procurement Workflow

## 9.1 Purchase Request requirement

After an MFR is approved, every purchase under the relevant account head should be initiated through a Purchase Request or Local Purchase Requisition.

This means MFR approval authorizes the budget, while the PR authorizes the specific purchase.

## 9.2 Existing Local Purchase Requisition format

- Requisition number

- Project name
- Account head

- Estimated cost
- Intended user or location
- Total estimated cost
- Amount in words
- Special instructions
- CEO approval
- Chairman approval

The sample is a historical office-rent requisition, but the structure can support general local purchase.

## 9.3 Current practical flow

```text
Approved MFR
    ↓
Purchase Need Identified
    ↓
Purchase Request Prepared
    ↓
Budget and Account Head Checked
    ↓
```

## 9.4 Procurement records that are not yet standardized

# 10. Fund Approval and Disbursement

## 10.1 Fund approval

Senior officials approve funds after:

- Reviewing the MFR
- Checking the budget
- Confirming the project and account head
- Reviewing the requested amount
- Reviewing annex details
- Confirming funding availability

## 10.2 Disbursement to project coordinator

After approval:

- The approved amount is released to the project coordinator.
- The release may be through cash, bank, or another authorized method.
- The coordinator becomes responsible for onward disbursement and reporting.

## 10.3 Field-level disbursement

Project coordinators send money to responsible field personnel, including:

- School head teachers
- Medical camp supervisors
- Other approved field officials

Methods include:

- Cash
- Bank deposit
- Authorized digital or mobile financial services when applicable

## 10.4 Field responsibility

The field recipient must:

- Use the money for the approved purpose
- Follow the approved account head
- Retain bills and vouchers
- Return unused funds or carry them forward only with approval
- Report additional needs
- Submit expenditure evidence
- Support project reporting

## 10.5 Current traceability gap

The existing files do not always provide a single identifier connecting:

- Annual budget
- MFR
- Additional MFR
- PR
- Disbursement
- Field recipient
- Expense
- Voucher
- Bank or cash transaction
- Monthly report

This is a central requirement for the future system.

HSF uses IOU entries when funds are advanced to:

# 12. Bills, Vouchers, and Expense Documentation

## 12.1 Requirement

Every expenditure must be supported by proper documentation.

Expected evidence includes:

- Bill
- Cash memo
- Invoice
- Receipt
- Payment voucher
- Acknowledgement
- Attendance sheet for events or training
- Participant list
- Delivery receipt
- Photograph when relevant
- Approval correspondence
- Bank or MFS proof
- Field confirmation

## 12.2 Month-end submission

At the end of each month:

1. Field teams close their bills and vouchers.
2. The documents are submitted to the project coordinator.
3. The coordinator checks the expenditure against the MFR.
4. Missing or invalid documents are followed up.
5. The coordinator prepares the monthly financial report.
6. The report is submitted to senior management and Finance.
7. The month is reviewed and closed.

## 12.3 Current evidence gaps

The uploaded ledger files contain transactions but do not consistently show:

- Voucher number
- Bill number
- MFR number
- PR number
- Vendor
- Recipient
- Document attachment
- Approval status
- Tax or VAT treatment
- Adjustment status

The future system must require these fields according to transaction type.

# 30. Findings from Current Records

## 30.1 Strengths

HSF already has:

- Annual planning
- Project budgeting
- Monthly MFR control
- Additional-fund approval
- Purchase-requisition practice
- Multi-bank operations
- Salary records
- Cash and bank transaction records
- Project and location classification
- School reporting
- Patient-level data
- Staff work plans
- Annual progress review
- Donor and sponsorship concepts
- Field supervision
- Audit and accountability intention

## 30.2 Main operational weaknesses

- Information is fragmented across many workbooks.
- Data is repeatedly entered in different formats.
- Current and historical structures are mixed.
- Some forms were copied from other programme models.
- There is no universal document number.
- MFR, PR, disbursement, voucher, and transaction are not linked end to end.
- Bank transfers can be confused with income or expense.
- IOUs are not fully controlled by ageing and settlement status.
- Account heads mix accounting and project dimensions.
- Formula errors exist.
- Dates and periods are inconsistent.
- Bank reconciliation is incomplete for one account.
- Patient data has weak validation and duplication risk.
- Project KPIs mix different units.
- Employee and beneficiary records do not have one shared master ID.
- Sensitive health, payroll, and bank data is stored in ordinary files.
- Approval communication may occur by phone or WhatsApp without a complete audit trail.
- There is no unified document repository.
- Reporting depends on individual spreadsheet knowledge.

# 32. Current-State Conclusion

HSF has a functioning operating model supported by experienced people, practical approval processes, field-level service delivery, and substantial Excel-based reporting.

The organization is not lacking processes. It is lacking a single controlled system that connects those processes.

The future Complete and Integrated Management System must preserve the core HSF control sequence:

> **Plan → Budget → MFR → Approval → PR → Disbursement → Activity → Bill/Voucher → Project Report → Financial Report → Reconciliation → Closure**

It must also connect this financial cycle with:

- Students
- HSF Graduates
- Patients
- Doctors
- Supervisors
- Teachers
- Staff
- Donors
- Events
- Training
- Locations
- Projects
- Bank accounts
- Documents
- Results

This document should be used as the current operational baseline for the next stage: **HSF Integrated Management System discovery, process validation, and product-requirement design**.
